# LinkedIn post: Two doors into Washington

Not published on the site. Attach the scatter (screenshot the first chart at 2x).

---

The U.S. midterms just ended, and "foreign money in elections" is back in the headlines. So I looked at the public data.

First, the legal point: foreign nationals and governments can't donate to U.S. campaigns. They have two legal ways to influence Washington instead:

🏢 Door 1: companies. A foreign firm's U.S. subsidiary can run a PAC, funded by its American employees. In the 2024 cycle, 109 of these PACs gave $23.7M. UBS's U.S. PAC alone gave $2.9M.

🤝 Door 2: hired agents. Governments, state companies and parties hire registered U.S. lobbyists and PR firms. They have to disclose this under the Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA).

Put each country on both axes and the dots split by which door it uses:

• Switzerland, France and the Netherlands go almost entirely through companies. The Netherlands: $0.8M through PACs (Transamerica, Philips, Heineken, Ahold Delhaize) and just 3 FARA clients.
• Saudi Arabia, Ukraine, the UAE and Qatar go through agents: dozens of FARA clients and no PAC money.
• Japan, the UK, Germany and Canada use both.

The pattern follows how each economy is built. Countries with big private multinationals reach Washington through their employees. Countries without big U.S. subsidiaries, often where the state runs the economy, hire lobbyists.

What this doesn't show:
– PAC money comes from Americans, and these PACs give to both parties (roughly 42% D / 58% R in 2024, per OpenSecrets).
– FARA counts clients, not dollars. The bulk data has no amounts.
– Companies only legally based abroad (Medtronic, Accenture) are left out. Airbus counts as French.

Part 2 looks at the other side: American donors to foreign-policy and diaspora PACs, same method.
