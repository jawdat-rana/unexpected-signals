## What to look for

- **The tide goes round the country.** High water reaches Hoek van Holland about an hour after Vlissingen, Den Helder after about 4 hours, and Delfzijl after about 9. By the time it gets to the Dollard, the next tide is already coming in at Vlissingen.
- **Storm Pia.** On the evening of 21 December the sea at Hoek van Holland rose to 3.13 metres above NAP, at 22:20. At 20:10 the Maeslantkering had swung shut: the first time it closed on its own for a storm since it opened in 1997. Behind it, the water at Maassluis stood at 1.87 metres, 1.26 metres lower than the sea. The surge then travelled north overnight: Delfzijl reached 4.12 metres at 06:30 the next morning.
- **The Rhine.** The flood wave crossed the border at Lobith, where the Rhine peaked at 14.32 metres above NAP at 03:00 on 28 December. It took almost two more days to reach Hagestein on the Lek.
- **The Maas came later and slower.** Its wave entered at Eijsden on 4 January and reached Venlo two days later and Heesbeen, near the end of the river, on 8 January.

## How it was made

Rijkswaterstaat publishes the measured water height of its gauges every 10 minutes as open data. We took 55 gauges along the coast, the Western Scheldt, the Rotterdam river mouth and the big rivers, and put them on one 10-minute clock. Each gauge is coloured by how far it stands above or below its own median on 18 and 19 December. A gauge is grey when it has no reading. The lines between gauges are straight, not the real river course, and their colour runs from one gauge's level to the next.

The peak levels in the replay match the ones in Rijkswaterstaat's storm surge report for Pia to the centimetre and the minute (Vlissingen, Hoek van Holland, Den Helder, Harlingen, Delfzijl). The Maeslantkering is shown shut from 20:10 on 21 December to 04:45 on 22 December, the times Rijkswaterstaat reported. The report doesn't give the time the Oosterscheldekering closed, so that comes from the water levels: the moment the sea side of the Roompot lock stood more than 1.2 metres above the Oosterschelde side for two hours or more. Rijkswaterstaat reported it open again at about 02:30.
